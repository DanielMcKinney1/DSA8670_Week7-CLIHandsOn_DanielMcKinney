Date: 10/17/2026



Question: Is house square footage affected by the year the house was built? Are newer houses, on average in this location, larger or smaller than older houses?



Paragraph: The question of whether square footage is reasonably affected by the year of construction was answered by two graphs: 1) a scatterplot with linear regression line of year-built by living area (sq ft) and 2) a column chart with the average square footage representing the y-axis and the year-built aggregated by decade. No hypothesis tests were performed. When analyzing the scatterplot, it can be seen that the regression model shows a slight but significant upwards slope, indicating a positive relationship between the 'new-ness' of the house and the size of the house. When analyzing the column chart, it can be seen that the houses built between 1870-1910 were on average larger than the houses built from 1920-1980. The houses built from 1990-2010 were on average larger than the 1920-1980 houses. This indicates that the relationship is not linear, but rather more akin to an inverse-parabolic shape, with the oldest and newest houses being the largest. The reason the linear regression saw a positive slope is that the 1990's and 2000's houses were much more frequent than the 1870-1910 houses, causing the linear relationship to appear positive. A non-linear smoothed model may better model the dependence structure between construction year and living area.
